# H03: The Dancing Plague of 1518

- **Story:** Strasbourg, July 1518 (history × psychology crossover)
- **Target length:** ~50 s (~150 words)
- **Title:** In 1518, a whole town couldn't stop dancing
- **Hook text on screen:** "They danced for weeks"

---

**HOOK (0–3 s)**
In 1518, a woman started dancing in the street. And she couldn't stop.

**BEAT 1: it spreads**
Strasbourg, July 1518. A woman known as Frau Troffea starts dancing and keeps going for days. Within weeks, dozens of people have joined her. Some accounts say hundreds.

**BEAT 2: the "cure"**
The city's solution? More dancing. Officials figured people had to dance it out of their system, so they opened up halls, built a stage, and hired musicians. It made it worse.

**BEAT 3: the end**
Some dancers collapsed from exhaustion. Finally the city banned public dancing and sent the dancers to a shrine of Saint Vitus to pray, and it slowly died out.

**ENDING**
So what was it? The leading theory is mass psychogenic illness. Famine, disease, extreme stress, and a local belief that a saint could curse you to dance. Fear spread through a crowd until their bodies acted it out. Follow for more history where the real cause was the human brain.

---

**Filming notes:** start the hook mid-sway, then stop and talk. The ending ties into the psych channel, so it's a good cross-promo later.

**Sources:** John Waller, *A Time to Dance, a Time to Die: The Extraordinary Story of the Dancing Plague of 1518* (2008). Death counts are disputed, so the script doesn't give one.
