# H04: The Boston Molasses Flood

- **Story:** North End, Boston, January 15, 1919
- **Target length:** ~50 s (~150 words)
- **Title:** A 25-foot wave of molasses hit Boston
- **Hook text on screen:** "Boston, 1919: death by molasses"

---

**HOOK (0–3 s)**
In 1919, a wave of molasses tore through Boston at 35 miles an hour.

**BEAT 1: the tank**
January 15th, 1919. In Boston's North End, a giant tank holding over two million gallons of molasses, used to make industrial alcohol, suddenly bursts.

**BEAT 2: the wave**
A wave up to 25 feet tall rolls through the streets. It knocks buildings off their foundations and bends the elevated train tracks. And in the January cold, the molasses got thicker as it cooled, so people caught in it couldn't get out. 21 people died, and about 150 were hurt.

**BEAT 3: the cover-up**
The company blamed anarchists. The court didn't buy it. The tank had never been properly tested, and it leaked so badly that the company painted it brown so the leaks wouldn't show.

**ENDING**
The disaster pushed Massachusetts to require engineers to sign off on structures like this. And locals said that on hot days, the North End smelled like molasses for decades.

---

**Filming notes:** keep the tone respectful in beat 2 (21 deaths). A jar of syrup or molasses as a prop works for the hook.

**Sources:** Stephen Puleo, *Dark Tide: The Great Boston Molasses Flood of 1919* (2003); Massachusetts Historical Society; the 2016 Harvard fluid-dynamics analysis (Nicole Sharp & Jordan Kennedy) on why the cold made it deadlier.
