**COLD OPEN**
Lee "Faker" Sang-hyeok won his first League of Legends World Championship in 2013. He was 17. He's now won six: 2013, 2015, 2016, 2023, 2024 and 2025. He's 30 years old, which in esports is basically ancient. And right now, at Worlds 2026 here in the United States, T1 is trying to win a fourth title in a row. There's a famous study that makes this look almost impossible.

**1. "OVER THE HILL AT 24"**
In 2014, researchers at Simon Fraser University analyzed thousands of players in the strategy game StarCraft 2. They measured something specific: how fast a player reacts to new information on screen and acts on it. They found that speed starts declining around age 24. It compared players of different ages in one game, so it's a snapshot, not a rule. But slowing with age is well documented across psychology. The paper's actual title is "Over the Hill at 24." In a game where milliseconds matter, that's a big deal. So by that math, Faker has been over the hill for six years.

**2. SO HOW IS HE STILL WINNING?**
The same study found something important: older players compensated. They used shortcuts and more efficient strategies to make up for the lost speed. And that connects to a classic chess study from 1973. Researchers William Chase and Herbert Simon showed chess masters and beginners a board for five seconds, then asked them to rebuild it. Masters crushed it, *if* the pieces came from a real game. When the pieces were placed randomly, most of their advantage disappeared. Later research found experts keep a smaller edge on random material, and a 2017 meta-analysis confirmed it across different fields. But the core idea has held up: experts see patterns, or "chunks," that beginners don't. Faker has seen more League of Legends situations than almost any human alive. My read: when a fight starts, a younger player may be reacting while Faker is recognizing.

**3. IS IT JUST PRACTICE?**
You've probably heard of the "10,000 hours" rule. The truth is messier. A big 2014 analysis by Brooke Macnamara and colleagues found that deliberate practice explains about a quarter of the differences in performance in games like chess. That's a lot, but it means three-quarters is something else: talent, age, coaching, the team around you, and the mental side. And the mental side is where Faker stands out.

**4. THE QUIET PART**
Faker is famous for how calm he is. He's lost world finals, in 2017 and 2022, and come back. He went through long stretches where people said he was done. Sports psychologists call the ability to stay steady under pressure "emotional regulation." Watch his face after a lost game. It barely changes. I can't tell you what's going on inside, and nobody has studied Faker in a lab. But it's hard to believe that hurts.

**OUTRO**
So the science isn't wrong. Raw speed really does fade. Faker is a reminder that speed is only one part of being great, and experience can cover part of the gap. That's good news for anyone who thinks they're "too old" to get good at something. You might get slower. You also get wiser. Do you think T1 gets the four-peat?
