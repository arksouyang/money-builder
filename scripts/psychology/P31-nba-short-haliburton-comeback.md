# P31: Your body heals faster than your confidence

- **Category:** NBA · **Format:** under 3 min, post as a YouTube Short too
- **Concepts:** fear of reinjury, kinesiophobia, psychological readiness
- **Target length:** ~2:15 (~320 words)
- **Title:** Why comebacks are harder than recovery
- **Thumbnail text:** "HEALED ≠ READY"

---

**HOOK**
Tyrese Haliburton waited more than a year to play basketball again. Four games later, he got hurt. And what research says about comebacks might surprise you: the hardest part often isn't the injury.

**1. THE SETBACK**
Haliburton tore his Achilles in June of 2025 and missed all of last season. He came back this fall, averaging about 22 points and 9 and a half assists. Then, four games in, he strained his left hamstring. It's a different injury, and it could cost him a couple of weeks. But imagine being him. You did all that work. And on game four, your body says, "Not so fast."

**2. THE SURPRISING NUMBER**
Researchers have spent years studying athletes returning from knee surgery, specifically ACL tears. A big review led by Clare Ardern found that about 81 percent of people get back to some kind of sport, but only about 65 percent return to their old level, and about 55 percent to competitive sport. Here's the part that surprised people. The most common reason given for playing less wasn't pain or a weak knee. It was fear of getting hurt again.

**3. FEAR HAS A NAME**
Clinicians call it kinesiophobia: fear of movement. And research on psychological readiness shows that how ready you feel predicts whether you return to your old level. The tissue heals on a schedule. Trust doesn't. You can have a perfect scan and still hesitate for a split second before you plant your foot. Now to be clear, that research is mostly on ACLs, not Achilles tendons. And Haliburton hasn't said any of this about himself. But it's a reminder of what a comeback really is.

**ENDING**
We talk about recovery like a straight line. It isn't. It's two lines: your body and your belief, and they don't move at the same speed. So if you're coming back from something, an injury, a breakup, a failed semester, and you're "better" but don't feel better, you're not doing it wrong. Your confidence is just on a different schedule.
