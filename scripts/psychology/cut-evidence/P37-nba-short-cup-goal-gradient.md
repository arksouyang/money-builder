# P37: Why the NBA invented a tournament in the middle of its season

- **Category:** NBA · **Format:** under 3 min, post as a YouTube Short too · **post during group play (Oct 30–early Dec)**
- **Concepts:** the goal-gradient effect, illusory progress
- **Target length:** ~1:45 (~270 words)
- **Title:** The coffee card trick behind the NBA Cup
- **Thumbnail text:** "THE COFFEE CARD TRICK"

---

**HOOK**
An NBA season is 82 games. Games in November can feel like they don't matter. So how do you make players and fans care? The answer might be sitting in your wallet: a coffee loyalty card.

**1. THE RATS AND THE CARDS**
Back in 1932, psychologist Clark Hull noticed rats ran faster the closer they got to food. He called it the goal gradient. In 2006, researchers tested it on people, with a coffee shop. One group got a card that needed 10 stamps for a free coffee. Another group got a card that needed 12 stamps, but with two already stamped. Both groups had to buy exactly 10 coffees. The second group finished faster. Same work. It just felt like they'd already started, and customers sped up as they got closer.

**2. THE CUP**
Now look at the NBA Cup. It started October 30th. Six groups of five teams. Four group games, which also count in the regular season. Win your group and you're into the knockout rounds. The final's December 11th in Indianapolis. And last year's winners reportedly got more than 500,000 dollars each. See what that does? Nothing about the games changed. It's still mostly the same schedule. But instead of a finish line that's 82 games away, there's one that's four games away. The league took a marathon and painted a finish line in the middle.

**ENDING**
You can do this to yourself. Don't plan to "write the paper" or "get in shape." Make the finish line close: one page, one workout, and put a stamp on it. Your brain doesn't run toward far away. It runs toward almost there. What's your next stamp?
