# P32: Why teams sometimes play better without their star

- **Category:** NBA · **Format:** under 3 min, post as a YouTube Short too
- **Concepts:** the Ewing Theory, social loafing, social compensation
- **Target length:** ~2:15 (~330 words)
- **Title:** The Ewing Theory, explained by psychology
- **Thumbnail text:** "BETTER WITHOUT HIM?"

---

**HOOK**
The Orlando Magic lost their leading scorer. Paolo Banchero went down with a high ankle sprain. So what did they do next? They walked into Madison Square Garden and beat the defending champions.

**1. THE GAME**
Down 10 in the fourth quarter on October 25th, Orlando exploded. Franz Wagner scored 21 of his 23 points in that quarter alone, and the Magic won the period 40 to 27. Without their star. Basketball fans have a name for this: the Ewing Theory. Writer Bill Simmons made it famous in 2001, crediting his friend Dave Cirilli. The idea is that some teams mysteriously play better when their best player is out. Fun fact: Simmons later admitted Patrick Ewing's own Knicks weren't actually a great example. So is it real?

**2. MANY HANDS**
There's real psychology near this. In 1979, Bibb Latané and his colleagues asked people to clap and shout as loudly as they could. When people believed five others were shouting with them, they produced about a third less noise than when they thought they were alone. It's called social loafing. When someone else can carry the load, we ease up without noticing. Now flip it. When your star's gone, there's nobody to defer to. Nobody's coming. Kipling Williams and Steven Karau found a related effect, social compensation: people can actually work harder when they expect their teammates won't deliver.

**3. BUT BE CAREFUL**
Here's the catch. We remember the games where a team surprised us, and forget all the times they just lost without their best player. That's confirmation bias. Banchero's a star, and Orlando is almost certainly better with him. One great quarter doesn't change that. The Ewing Theory is mostly a story. But inside it is something true.

**ENDING**
Nobody steps up until there's room to step up. If you've ever watched a quiet person take over when the loud one left the room, you've seen it. So who's the person in your life who only shows up when nobody else will? Tell them. And tell me.
