# P11: Why the Roy kids can never walk away from Logan

- **Category:** TV
- **Concepts:** intermittent reinforcement (Skinner), traumatic bonding, conditional regard from parents
- **Target length:** ~3:45 (~560 words) · regular video · spoilers for *Succession*
- **Title:** Succession is a psychology experiment, and the kids are the rats
- **Thumbnail text:** "THE SLOT MACHINE DAD"

---

**COLD OPEN**
Logan Roy insults his kids, betrays them, humiliates them in front of the whole company. And every season, they come crawling back. Kendall, Shiv, Roman: smart, rich, adult people who could walk away any day. They don't. Viewers yell "just leave!" at the screen. But psychology has a pretty precise explanation for why they can't, and it's the same thing that makes slot machines work.

**1. THE PIGEON EXPERIMENT**
In the 1950s, B.F. Skinner trained animals by rewarding them for pressing a lever. He found something strange. If you reward them *every* time, they learn fast but quit fast once the reward stops. If you reward them *unpredictably*, sometimes after 3 presses, sometimes after 30, they press like crazy and keep pressing long after the rewards stop. That's called a variable-ratio schedule, or intermittent reinforcement. It's the most addictive reward pattern there is. Casinos built an industry on it.

**2. LOGAN IS A SLOT MACHINE**
Now look at Logan. He doesn't give his kids love. He gives them *maybe*. Maybe you're the successor. Maybe you're "number one boy." Then it's gone. Then, just when they're about to give up, a crumb: a hand on the shoulder, a "you did good." Every one of the Roy kids is pulling the lever, waiting for the jackpot of being chosen. And because the payout is random, they can never stop pulling.

**3. WHY IT BONDS YOU HARDER**
Psychologists Donald Dutton and Susan Painter studied what they called "traumatic bonding." People in relationships that swing between mistreatment and affection often form *stronger* attachments, not weaker ones. The relief after cruelty feels like love. It's not the kids being stupid. It's their nervous systems being trained.

**4. LOVE WITH CONDITIONS**
There's one more layer. Researchers Avi Assor, Guy Roth and Edward Deci studied "conditional regard," parents who give love only when kids perform. Their kids did push themselves hard, but they also felt more resentment toward their parents, and their self-worth swung up and down with every success and failure. That's the Roy kids in one sentence. Kendall's whole identity rises and falls on whether Dad approves today.

**5. THE LINE THAT EXPLAINS IT**
Near the end, Logan tells his kids, "I love you, but you are not serious people." Even his "I love you" comes with a "but." That's conditional regard in nine words.

**OUTRO**
The reason *Succession* hits so hard is that a lot of people recognize it. A parent, a boss, an ex who was amazing just often enough. If you've ever stayed somewhere because of the good days you were waiting for, that's intermittent reinforcement, and knowing the name for it is the first step to noticing it. Who do you think came closest to actually breaking free? I'll say it: none of them.
