# P30: What happens to a team after a player gets badly hurt

- **Category:** NBA · **Format:** under 3 min, post as a YouTube Short too
- **Concepts:** athletic identity, mood linkage, emotional contagion
- **Target length:** ~2:15 (~340 words)
- **Title:** The injury you can't see on the replay
- **Thumbnail text:** "NEXT MAN UP?"

---

**HOOK**
Sports has a phrase for when a player goes down: "next man up." It sounds tough. Psychologically, it's almost impossible.

**1. WHAT HAPPENED**
On October 25th, with just over a minute left in a win over the Clippers, Thunder star Jalen Williams went down. He broke his leg, dislocated his ankle, and needs surgery. He was stretchered off while the Oklahoma City crowd chanted his name, and he gave them a thumbs up. After the game, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander teared up on TV. His coach said what any honest person would say: they weren't doing great. That's a real loss for a real person, so let's talk about what's happening beneath the box score.

**2. THE PLAYER**
Sport psychologists study something called athletic identity: how much of who you are is tied to being an athlete. In a 1993 paper, Britton Brewer and his colleagues argued it can be both your strength and your weak spot. And research links a stronger athletic identity with a harder emotional reaction to injury. When your body is your job, your purpose and your community, an injury isn't just physical.

**3. THE TEAM**
Then there's everyone else. In 2000, psychologist Peter Totterdell had professional cricket players rate their mood three times a day during a match. A player's mood tracked the mood of his teammates, even after accounting for how the game was going. Moods spread through a team. So a locker room doesn't go through this one person at a time. It goes through it together. Three days after the injury, the Thunder lost to Dallas by 24. I can't tell you that's why. One game isn't evidence. But it would be strange if a team felt nothing.

**ENDING**
His coach said something that I think is exactly right: you have to let yourself feel the way you feel. "Next man up" tells you what to do on the court. It can't tell you what to do with that. If someone you work or play with is hurting, you're allowed to be affected. That's not weakness. That's a team.
