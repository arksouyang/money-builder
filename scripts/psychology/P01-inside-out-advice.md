# P01: Why "have you tried..." makes sad people feel worse

- **Concept:** matching hypothesis (Cohen & Wills, 1985), three types of support (Lecture 6)
- **Pop culture:** *Inside Out* (2015), Bing Bong scene
- **Target length:** ~45 s (~140 words)
- **Title:** The worst thing to say to a sad friend
- **Hook text on screen:** "Stop giving your friends advice"

---

**HOOK (0–3 s)**
The worst thing you can give a sad friend is a really good solution.

**BEAT 1: the idea**
Psychologists call this the matching hypothesis. Support only helps when it matches what the person actually needs. Give advice to someone who wanted comfort, and you can make their stress *worse*.

**BEAT 2: the example**
Pixar put this in *Inside Out*. Bing Bong watches his rocket get dumped into the Memory Dump and falls apart. Joy does what most of us do: tries to cheer him up and get him moving. Nothing works. Then Sadness just sits next to him and says, basically, "That really sucks. You loved that." And he gets back up.

**BEAT 3: the three types**
There are three kinds of support. Emotional: "I'm here." Informational: advice. Instrumental: actually doing something, like driving you somewhere. All three are good. The wrong one at the wrong time isn't.

**ENDING**
So before you fix it, ask: "Do you want advice, or do you want me to just listen?" Comment which one *you* usually need.
