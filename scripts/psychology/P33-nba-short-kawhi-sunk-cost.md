# P33: Toronto bet big on Kawhi. Was that smart, or sunk cost?

- **Category:** NBA · **Format:** under 3 min, post as a YouTube Short too · **recheck his status before filming**
- **Concepts:** sunk cost fallacy, escalation of commitment, uncertainty and stress
- **Target length:** ~2:15 (~350 words)
- **Title:** Why we can't quit bad bets
- **Thumbnail text:** "$115 MILLION?"

---

**HOOK**
Kawhi Leonard is 35. His knees have a long history. And this year, the Raptors traded for him, gave up players and picks, then agreed to a two-year extension worth a projected 115 million dollars. Then he was a late scratch with knee swelling. Here's the psychological trap every one of us falls into.

**1. THE TICKET STUDY**
In the 1980s, Hal Arkes and Catherine Blumer ran a sneaky experiment. People buying season theater tickets were randomly given discounts. The ones who paid full price went to more shows. The show was the same. The only difference was what they'd already spent. That's the sunk cost fallacy. Money you've spent is gone either way, but it still drives your next decision.

**2. IT MAY EVEN HAPPEN IN THE NBA**
In 1995, Barry Staw and Ha Hoang found that NBA teams gave higher draft picks more minutes and more time in the league, even accounting for how well they played. Teams seemed to stick with the players they'd paid the most for. But here's where it's honest science. Later economists found a weaker effect, and a 2015 study with a sharper method found no effect at all. So the jury's still out on whether pro teams actually do this.

**3. SO WHAT ABOUT TORONTO?**
To be fair, this might be a smart bet. Leonard won them a championship in 2019, and imaging reportedly showed no acute injury this time. But watch the logic. "We already gave up so much, so we have to extend him" is a sunk cost argument. The only fair question is: knowing what we know today, is this the best use of the money? That's my read, not a fact about how the Raptors decided.

**4. THE WAITING**
Raptors fans get a bonus. A 2016 study in *Nature Communications* found people were most stressed not when a shock was certain, but when they really couldn't tell if it was coming. Is he playing tonight? Nobody knows. That's the Kawhi experience.

**ENDING**
So next time you're stuck in a bad class, a bad relationship or a bad movie, ask this: if I were starting fresh today, would I choose this? What are you still doing just because you already paid for it?
