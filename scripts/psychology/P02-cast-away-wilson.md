# P02: Wilson the volleyball was the smartest thing in Cast Away

- **Concept:** loneliness vs. social isolation, social ties and mortality (Lecture 6); anthropomorphism under loneliness (outside the notes)
- **Pop culture:** *Cast Away* (2000)
- **Target length:** ~50 s (~145 words)
- **Title:** Why Tom Hanks talked to a volleyball (psychology)
- **Hook text on screen:** "Wilson saved his life"

---

**HOOK (0–3 s)**
Tom Hanks talking to a volleyball in *Cast Away* isn't him going crazy. It's his brain trying to keep him alive.

**BEAT 1: the brain**
When people are lonely, they start seeing minds in things that don't have them. Pets, gadgets, a ball with a handprint for a face. Studies have found lonely people are more likely to give human traits to objects. Your brain wants connection so badly it builds one.

**BEAT 2: why it matters**
And it has a good reason. A huge review of studies found strong social ties predict survival about as much as quitting smoking, and more than exercise or avoiding obesity.

**BEAT 3: the twist**
But being alone and feeling lonely aren't the same thing. You can live alone and be fine, or sit in a packed lecture hall and feel invisible. Both feeling lonely and being cut off are linked to worse health.

**ENDING**
So Wilson wasn't a sign Chuck was losing it. Wilson is part of why he made it. Go text your Wilson.
