# P34: Five straight wins doesn't mean what you think

- **Category:** NBA · **Format:** under 3 min, post as a YouTube Short too · **update record before filming**
- **Concepts:** regression to the mean, law of small numbers
- **Target length:** ~2:00 (~290 words)
- **Title:** The mistake every sports fan makes in October
- **Thumbnail text:** "5-0 MEANS NOTHING?"

---

**HOOK**
The Houston Rockets started 5-0, their best start since 2014. [UPDATE BEFORE FILMING] Before you crown them, try this. Take 30 coins and flip each one five times. On average, about one coin will land heads five times in a row. Pure luck.

**1. THE MATH**
Five heads in a row is one in 32. The NBA has 30 teams. So even if every team were exactly equal, you'd expect roughly one team to start 5-0 by chance alone. Now, the Rockets aren't coins. They won 52 games last year, and they've got Kevin Durant and Fred VanVleet, back from a torn ACL. They might be great. I'm just saying five games can't tell you that. Five games is about 6 percent of a season.

**2. THE LAW OF SMALL NUMBERS**
In 1971, Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman showed that even trained scientists trust tiny samples way too much. They jokingly called it a belief in the law of small numbers. We see a streak, and our brain writes a story: they've got chemistry, they want it more.

**3. WHAT HAPPENS NEXT**
Then comes regression to the mean. Extreme results tend to be followed by more ordinary ones. Kahneman tells a great story about this. Flight instructors in Israel swore that yelling at pilots worked, because after a terrible landing, pilots improved. And after praise, they got worse. But nothing was causing that. A terrible landing is usually partly bad luck, so the next one is better either way. The same is true for your hot start, and your cold start.

**ENDING**
So if you had one bad week, one bad test, one bad date, don't rewrite your whole story. And if you had one great one, same thing. It takes more than five games to know who you are. Who's your October fraud? Tell me in the comments.
