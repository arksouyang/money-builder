# P36: Stephen Curry has nothing left to win. So why is he still doing this?

- **Category:** NBA · **Format:** under 3 min, post as a YouTube Short too
- **Concepts:** intrinsic motivation, the overjustification effect, self-determination theory
- **Target length:** ~2:00 (~300 words)
- **Title:** The motivation trick that keeps legends going
- **Thumbnail text:** "44 AT 38"

---

**HOOK**
Stephen Curry is 38 years old. He has four championships and two MVPs. His team had started 0-3. And on October 28th in Minnesota, he scored 44 points, and went 16-for-16 at the line. That's his 15th 40-point game since turning 35, tied with LeBron for the most ever. So what's motivating a man who's already won everything?

**1. THE MAGIC MARKERS**
In 1973, Mark Lepper, David Greene and Richard Nisbett went to a preschool and found kids who already loved drawing. They split them up. Some were told, "Draw, and you'll get a Good Player award." Some got a surprise award afterward. Some got nothing. Later, during free play, the kids who'd been promised a reward drew noticeably less than the others. The kids hadn't gotten worse. The reward changed the reason. It turned play into work.

**2. WHY THAT HAPPENS**
It's called the overjustification effect. Your brain watches your own behavior and asks why you're doing it. If there's a big reward attached, it answers, "Must be for the reward." Take the reward away, and the motivation goes with it. Edward Deci and Richard Ryan built this into self-determination theory. Long-term drive comes from three things: choosing it yourself, getting better at it, and doing it with people you care about.

**3. WHAT THAT MEANS FOR A LEGEND**
Now, I can't see inside Curry's head. But think about the athletes who stay great late in their careers. If it was only ever about the ring or the money, the fuel runs out when you get them. If it's about the craft, about making the next shot, there's no finish line. You can't complete it.

**ENDING**
So if something you used to love started to feel like a chore once it became a job or a grade, that's not you getting lazy. That's the reward taking over the reason. Find the part you'd do for free. What's yours?
