---

**HOOK**
ESPN's preseason index ranked the Memphis Grizzlies 24th in the league. They'd just lost Ja Morant. Then they started 4-0. [UPDATE BEFORE FILMING] And a guy who'd been a sixth man dropped 45 on opening night. Suddenly, everybody's a Grizzlies fan. Why does that happen?

**1. THE STORY**
Ty Jerome finished third in Sixth Man of the Year voting in 2025, then signed with Memphis on a three-year deal worth about 28 million. Not exactly superstar money. On opening night, he scored a career-high 45, breaking Morant's old franchise record for an opener. Then he hit 14 of 14 free throws in a 31-point game. It's a great story, and your brain is built to love it.

**2. THE STUDY**
In 2007, psychologist Joseph Vandello and his colleagues ran a series of studies on this. Just describing a competitor as the underdog made people like them more, even when they had nothing riding on it. In one study, people assumed the underdog was trying harder, and that perception of effort explained why they liked them. We don't just pity underdogs. We believe they've earned it.

**3. THE CATCH**
And here's the twist. Low expectations alone weren't enough. In the fourth study, when a struggling team was shown to have more resources than its opponent, the support faded. Fans wanted the win to feel deserved. Vandello's take is that rooting for the underdog is a small way of pushing back against unfairness.

**ENDING**
That means the underdog effect has an expiration date. If Memphis keeps winning, they stop being a story and become a target. So enjoy it while it lasts. What's the underdog you'll never stop rooting for?
