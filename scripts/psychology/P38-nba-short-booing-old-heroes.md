# P38: Why fans boo the players they used to love

- **Category:** NBA · **Format:** under 3 min, post as a YouTube Short too · **best posted before Jan 21 (Sixers at Celtics)**
- **Concepts:** the black sheep effect, basking in reflected glory, social identity
- **Target length:** ~2:00 (~310 words)
- **Title:** Why sports fans turn on their heroes
- **Thumbnail text:** "WE → THEY"

---

**HOOK**
When LeBron went back to Cleveland in 2010, fans who'd adored him for seven years booed him like a villain. Same with Kevin Durant in Oklahoma City. On January 21st, Jaylen Brown goes back to Boston. Will fans boo him? Psychology has a way of predicting it.

**1. WE WON. THEY LOST.**
In 1976, Robert Cialdini and his colleagues studied college football fans. After a win, students were more likely to wear school gear, and to say "we won." After a loss, it became "they lost." He called it basking in reflected glory. A team isn't just something you watch. It's a piece of who you are. That means when a star leaves, it doesn't feel like a job change. It feels like someone walking out on us.

**2. THE BLACK SHEEP**
In 1988, José Marques and his colleagues asked Belgian students to judge likable and unlikable people, some Belgian, some not. You'd expect people to go easy on their own. But unlikable Belgians got rated more harshly than unlikable outsiders. They called it the black sheep effect. We're harder on our own, because one of us going bad threatens what "us" means. A stranger on a rival team is just an opponent. A former hero is a traitor.

**3. SO WILL BOSTON BOO?**
Here's the key: that reaction depends on whether someone chose to leave. LeBron and Durant left on their own. Brown was traded, and he says he never demanded it. If fans believe he was sent away, he's still one of us, and he might get a standing ovation. If fans believe he wanted out, he's the black sheep. Same player. Same career. Different story in the crowd's head. That's why the trade rumors matter so much.

**ENDING**
Booing isn't really about basketball. It's a crowd protecting its identity. Next time you're furious at someone for leaving, ask: did they hurt me, or did they hurt the story I tell about us? Would you boo?
