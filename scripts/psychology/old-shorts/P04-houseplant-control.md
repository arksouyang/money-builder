# P04: The houseplant study

- **Concept:** control and predictability, perceived control (Lecture 7: Langer & Rodin, Visintainer)
- **Real life:** why stress you can't control hits harder (exams, bosses, group projects)
- **Target length:** ~50 s (~150 words)
- **Title:** They gave old people a plant and fewer of them died
- **Hook text on screen:** "1 plant. Half the deaths."

---

**HOOK (0–3 s)**
Scientists gave nursing home residents a houseplant. A year and a half later, half as many of them had died.

**BEAT 1: the setup**
In 1976, psychologists Ellen Langer and Judith Rodin split a nursing home by floor. One group was told: you're in charge. Pick a plant, take care of it, choose your movie night. The other group got a plant too, but the staff did everything for them.

**BEAT 2: the result**
Within three weeks, the "in charge" group was happier, more alert, more social. It was a small study, but eighteen months later about 15% of them had died, compared with about 30% of the other group.

**BEAT 3: the rats**
Same pattern in rats: two groups get the exact same shocks, but one group can turn them off. The ones with no control fought off tumors way less.

**ENDING**
Stress isn't the whole problem. Stress you can't control is. So when everything feels out of your hands, find one small thing that isn't. Even if it's a plant.
