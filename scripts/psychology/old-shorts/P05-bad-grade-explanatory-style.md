# P05: How you explain a bad grade predicts how long you live

- **Concept:** explanatory style (Peterson & Seligman), globality and mortality (Lecture 7)
- **Everyday struggle:** failing an exam
- **Target length:** ~45 s (~140 words)
- **Title:** The 3 sentences your brain says after you fail
- **Hook text on screen:** "It's me. It's forever. It's everything."

---

**HOOK (0–3 s)**
How you explain a bad grade might predict how long you live.

**BEAT 1: the pessimist**
Psychologists call it explanatory style: how you explain *why* bad things happen. Fail an exam, and a pessimistic brain says three things. "It's me." "It's forever." "It's everything."

**BEAT 2: the optimist**
An optimistic brain says: "That test was brutal." "I was exhausted that day." "It's one class." Same grade. Totally different story.

**BEAT 3: the scary part**
And "it's everything" is the dangerous one. A study that followed people for decades found the ones who explained bad events as wrecking *everything* died earlier, especially men, and especially from accidents.

**ENDING**
You don't have to fake being positive. Just catch the word "everything" and shrink it. "I failed this" is a fact. "I fail at everything" is a prediction, and it's usually wrong.

---

**Filming notes:** hold up a (fake or real) graded paper. Pop each of the three sentences on screen as text. Good one to film right after a midterm.

**Sources:**

- Peterson, C., Seligman, M. E. P., Yurko, K. H., Martin, L. R., & Friedman, H. S. (1998). Catastrophizing and untimely death. *Psychological Science*, 9(2), 127–130. (Terman life-cycle study.)
