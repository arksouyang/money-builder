# P03: The personality that doubles heart disease risk (it's Carmy)

- **Concept:** Type A behavior pattern, hostility (Lecture 7)
- **Pop culture:** *The Bear* (FX), Carmy
- **Target length:** ~50 s (~150 words)
- **Title:** Heart doctors predicted heart attacks from their chairs
- **Hook text on screen:** "The chairs gave it away"

---

**HOOK (0–3 s)**
Two heart doctors figured out who'd get heart disease by looking at their waiting room chairs.

**BEAT 1: the story**
As the story goes, an upholsterer came to fix the chairs in cardiologists Friedman and Rosenman's office and noticed something weird: only the front edges were worn out. Their heart patients were literally sitting on the edge of their seats.

**BEAT 2: Type A**
That became the "Type A" personality: hurried, competitive, tense. Basically Carmy from *The Bear*. Early studies found Type A people had about double the risk of heart disease.

**BEAT 3: the real culprit**
But later research found the dangerous part isn't ambition or being busy. It's hostility: constant anger and distrust. In one study, people high in hostility were more likely to get heart disease and to die over the next 25 years.

**ENDING**
The good news: it's trainable. In one small trial, hostile patients who got anger training ran up way smaller hospital bills than ones who didn't. So be driven. Just stop yelling "Yes, Chef" at yourself.

**Filming notes:** sit on the edge of your chair for the hook, then lean back for the ending. No clips from the show.
